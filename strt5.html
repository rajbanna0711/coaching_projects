Bhagat Singh Sandhu

1907 - 1931

Shaheed-e-Azam

Bhagat Singh (28 September 1907 - 23 March 1931) was a charismatic Indian revolutionary who became a symbol of youthful defiance and courage in the struggle for India's independence. Born in a Sikh family in Banga village, Punjab (now in Pakistan), he was drawn to revolutionary nationalism from a young age, deeply affected by incidents like the Jallianwala Bagh massacre . An atheist and socialist, his ideology was heavily influenced by Marxist and anarchist thought, and he was a key member of the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA). He is best known for avenging the death of Lala Lajpat Rai by killing British police officer John Saunders in 1928, and for a symbolic bombing of the Central Legislative Assembly in 1929. He used his subsequent trial and jail time to spread his revolutionary message. His execution at the age of 23 made him a martyr, and he remains one of India's most enduring folk heroes, often referred to as Shaheed-e-Azam (The Great Martyr).
